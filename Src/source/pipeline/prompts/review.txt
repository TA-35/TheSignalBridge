Return JSON matching the schema you were given.

`summary` - two sentences at most, and never empty. What is this message
actually doing, and what does it want? Write it for the reader, not as a
description of the text.

`claims` - each one needs `field` (exactly one of the values below), `text`
(words quoted exactly from the message), `span_hint` (roughly where those
words start - an estimate is fine), and `reading`.

  purpose  required_action  responsible_person  deadline  priority  context

`reading` is the whole point of a claim. It says what those words mean for
the person who received them - the assumption behind the phrasing, the thing
a literal reader would miss. "The deadline is Friday" is not a reading; it is
the quote again. "Friday is stated, but not a time - if they mean close of
business you have until 5, and if they mean before their 9am review you do
not" is a reading.

Make a claim only where you have something to say about it. Four is plenty;
one good one beats four restatements. Never pad - a quote you cannot find in
the message fails verification and the whole answer is discarded.

`missing` - which of those same six fields this message leaves unsettled for
the reader, and the `question` that would settle each. Judge this yourself
from the message; the extractor's list above is a keyword guess and it is
often wrong, both ways. A field you made a claim about is settled - do not
also list it as missing. A field nobody needs here is not missing either: a
thank-you note with no deadline is not missing a deadline. If the message
genuinely settles everything that matters, return an empty list.

Write `question` as the reader would type it to the sender - "Is Thursday
end of day, or do you need it before your morning review?" - not as a label.

If what you have to say about a field is that the message does not settle it,
that belongs in `missing`, not in `claims`. A claim reading "the sender does
not specify who is responsible" is a gap wearing a claim's clothes: it sends
the reader looking for an answer in the half of the page that is meant to
hold answers. Put it in `missing` and let the question carry it.

`unstated` - real questions the reader could ask, in their own words. Never
field names: not "responsible_person" but "Who else is expecting this, or is
it just me?" Each one should be something they could paste into a reply.
Keep these wider than `missing`: that list is about the six fields, this one
is for anything else worth asking. Do not repeat a question from `missing`.

Never name a person or a company that the message does not name. Not as an
example, not as a placeholder, not in a question. If you need to refer to
someone the message leaves unnamed, say "whoever owns this" or "the person
they mean". A name that is not in the message fails verification and the
whole answer is discarded.

Output the JSON object alone. No markdown fences, no preamble.
